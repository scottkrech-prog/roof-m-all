Storm damage roofing

Storm damage roof inspections after wind, hail, and heavy rain.

Roof-M-All helps Charlotte-area homeowners inspect, document, and understand roof damage after storms without promising outcomes the roof inspection cannot control.

Request FREE EstimateCall 704-631-9936
Direct answer

What homeowners should know about storm damage roofing.

After a storm, the first job is to determine whether the roof has visible wind, hail, water, flashing, gutter, or siding damage that should be documented.

Roof-M-All can inspect the roof and exterior, explain what is visible, photograph concerns, and help the homeowner understand repair, replacement, or insurance next steps.

Roof-M-All serves Charlotte, Mint Hill, Matthews, Weddington, Waxhaw, Marvin, and nearby communities from its Mint Hill base.

When to call about storm damage roofing

  • New leaks after a storm
  • Creased, missing, or lifted shingles
  • Dented gutters, vents, or soft metals
  • Shingle granules washed into gutters or downspouts
  • Branches, debris impact, or visible exterior damage

Process

How Roof-M-All approaches the first conversation.

1. Listen to the problem

Start with what the homeowner is seeing: leak, storm damage, age, exterior wear, project planning, or urgent concern.

2. Inspect and document

The practical next step is looking at the affected area and documenting visible conditions so options are clear.

3. Explain next steps

FAQs

Storm damage roofing questions.

Should I call a roofer before my insurance company?

Many homeowners start with a roof inspection so they understand what is visible before deciding whether to file a claim. Roof-M-All can document conditions but cannot guarantee insurance approval.

What should I photograph after roof storm damage?

Photograph interior leaks, ceiling stains, exterior debris, gutter dents, visible missing shingles, and the date/time context. Avoid climbing onto the roof if it is unsafe.

Does storm damage always require replacement?

No. Some damage may be repairable, while widespread damage may justify discussing replacement. The inspection and insurance process determine the next step.

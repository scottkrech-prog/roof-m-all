Storm damage insurance guide

Storm damage roof insurance claim process for Charlotte-area homeowners.

If wind, hail, or heavy rain may have damaged your roof, the safest first step is documentation and inspection — not guessing from the driveway or climbing onto a wet roof.

Request Storm InspectionStorm Damage Roofing
Direct answer

What should you do after possible storm damage?

Document visible evidence, keep the storm date in mind, and schedule a roof inspection before deciding whether to file an insurance claim. A contractor can inspect and document roof conditions, but the insurance carrier decides coverage under the policy.

Roof-M-All helps homeowners identify visible wind, hail, leak, flashing, gutter, siding, and exterior damage so the next step is based on what is actually present.

Important insurance note: Roof-M-All can inspect, photograph, and explain visible roof conditions. Roof-M-All cannot guarantee claim approval, coverage, payment, or replacement. Those decisions belong to the insurance carrier and policy.
Step-by-step

A practical storm damage response checklist.

Use these steps before, during, and after the roof inspection.

Look for safe interior and ground-level signs of damage.

Check ceilings, attic areas if safe, gutters, downspouts, yard debris, and visible roof edges.

Photograph leaks, stains, debris, gutter dents, missing shingles, and exterior damage.

Photos help preserve what happened and when, especially if repairs or cleanup are needed quickly.

Schedule a professional roof inspection before climbing on the roof yourself.

Avoid climbing on a damaged or wet roof. Ask for photo documentation and plain-English findings.

Review the inspection findings and decide whether a claim conversation is warranted.

Not every storm issue becomes a claim. Start with what the inspection can actually show.

If filing a claim, keep photos, dates, notes, and adjuster communication organized.

Organization helps homeowners have a clearer conversation with the carrier and adjuster.

Use the repair or replacement scope to plan next steps with the contractor and carrier.

The final scope may be repair, replacement, or monitoring depending on damage and coverage.

Common signs

Signs a Charlotte roof may need storm inspection.

Roof and exterior clues

  • Missing, lifted, or creased shingles
  • Granules washed into gutters
  • Dented gutters, vents, or soft metals
  • Tree limbs or impact debris
  • New siding or fascia damage

Interior clues

  • New ceiling stains
  • Water dripping after rain
  • Musty attic or wet insulation signs
  • Stains near walls, vents, or chimneys
  • Leaks that only appear during wind-driven rain

FAQs

Storm damage claim questions homeowners ask.

What should I do first after a hail or wind storm?

First, stay safe and document what you can see from the ground or inside the home. Photograph leaks, stains, debris, gutter dents, missing shingles, and storm timing. Then schedule a roof inspection if there may be damage.

Can Roof-M-All tell me whether insurance will approve my roof claim?

No roofing contractor can guarantee insurance approval. Roof-M-All can inspect and document visible roof and exterior conditions, but coverage decisions belong to the insurance carrier and policy.

Should I wait if there is no active leak?

No. Hail or wind damage can exist without an immediate indoor leak. If the storm was severe or nearby homes were affected, an inspection can help you understand whether there is visible damage.

What does an adjuster look for?

Adjusters commonly review visible damage, storm date context, roof surface conditions, collateral damage, policy coverage, age, and whether repair or replacement is justified under the policy.
